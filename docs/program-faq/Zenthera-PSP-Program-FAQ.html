Zenthera Patient Support Program

Frequently Asked Questions · Version 1.0 · November 2026

FICTIONAL DEMO CONTENT. Zenthera, Zenthera Therapeutics, the Zenthera Patient Support Program, CareLane Specialty Pharmacy, all amounts, phone numbers and rules in this document are invented for a software demonstration. This document gives no medical advice. Questions about dosing, side effects or treatment must go to the patient’s prescriber.

1. About the program

Q1. What is the Zenthera Patient Support Program?

The Zenthera Patient Support Program (PSP) helps patients who are prescribed Zenthera 50 mg or Zenthera 100 mg. A Patient Services Coordinator helps with enrollment, insurance questions, copay assistance, shipments and refills. The program is free for patients.

Q2. Who runs the program?

The program is run by Zenthera Therapeutics through its patient services team. Medication is shipped by CareLane Specialty Pharmacy.

Q3. What does the program not do?

Coordinators do not give medical advice, do not change prescriptions and do not decide treatment. These questions go to the patient’s prescriber.

2. Eligibility and enrollment

Q4. Who can enroll?

A patient can enroll if they have a valid prescription for Zenthera 50 mg or Zenthera 100 mg, live in the United States, and give consent to join the program. Patients with commercial insurance, Medicare, Medicaid or no insurance can all enroll; the services available depend on the insurance type (see section 3).

Q5. What information is needed to enroll?

Full name, date of birth, the prescribed therapy (Zenthera 50 mg or 100 mg), insurance type, and the patient’s consent. A mobile phone number and the prescriber’s name are recommended so the coordinator can send refill reminders and contact the prescriber’s office.

Q6. Can a caregiver enroll a patient?

Yes. A caregiver can provide the details, but the patient (or their legal representative) must give consent.

Q7. How long does enrollment take?

Enrollment is recorded as soon as the coordinator submits it, and the patient receives an enrollment number that starts with ENR-. The benefits check with the insurer usually takes 2 to 3 business days.

Q8. Can a patient enroll twice?

No. A patient can have only one open enrollment. If an enrollment already exists, the coordinator updates it instead of creating a new one.

3. Cost, insurance and copay assistance

Q9. How does copay assistance work?

Patients with commercial insurance may pay as little as $10 per monthly fill. The program covers up to $15,000 per calendar year. When the yearly maximum is reached, the coordinator explains the remaining options.

Q10. Can patients with Medicare or Medicaid use the copay program?

No. Copay assistance cannot be used with Medicare, Medicaid or other government insurance. The coordinator can share a list of independent charitable foundations that may help with costs.

Q11. What help is available for patients without insurance?

Uninsured patients may qualify for the Zenthera Free Medication Program if their household income is at or below 400% of the federal poverty level. The coordinator helps complete the application.

Q12. Does the program help with prior authorization?

Yes. The coordinator runs a benefits investigation and helps the prescriber’s office with prior authorization paperwork and appeals. The coordinator cannot guarantee that an insurer will approve coverage.

4. Shipments and refills

Q13. How is the medication delivered?

CareLane Specialty Pharmacy ships a 30-day supply to the patient’s home, with free standard shipping. A signature may be needed on delivery.

Q14. When are refills sent?

The next refill is due 30 days after the last delivered shipment. The program sends a refill reminder by text message 7 days before the due date, if the patient gave a mobile number.

Q15. What happens if a shipment is delayed?

Contact the coordinator. The coordinator checks the shipment status with the pharmacy and logs a service case so the delay is tracked until the shipment arrives.

Q16. What should a patient do if they miss a dose or run out of medication?

Questions about missed doses must go to the prescriber. The coordinator can check whether a refill is due or delayed and contact the pharmacy, but does not advise on dosing.

5. Side effects and safety

Q17. What should a patient do if they think they have a side effect?

Contact the prescriber. In an emergency, call 911. Patients can also tell their coordinator; the coordinator records it as a possible adverse event so that the Zenthera safety team can review it. Coordinators do not give medical advice about side effects.

Q18. Does the coordinator report side effects to a regulator?

The coordinator only records the report for review by the safety team. The safety team decides on any follow-up.

6. Contact, privacy and leaving the program

Q19. How can patients contact the program?

Call the program line at 1-800-555-0142, Monday to Friday, 8 am to 8 pm Eastern Time. Outside these hours, leave a message and a coordinator calls back on the next business day.

Q20. How is patient information used?

Patient information is used only to run the program: enrollment, insurance support, shipments, refill reminders and safety reporting. It is not sold or used for marketing without separate consent.

Q21. Can a patient leave the program?

Yes, at any time, by telling the coordinator or calling the program line. Leaving the program does not affect the patient’s prescription or their care from the prescriber.
